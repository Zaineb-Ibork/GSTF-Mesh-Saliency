Abstract

Mesh saliency predicts which vertices of a 3D surface attract human attention. Its core difficulty is that perceptual importance is inherently non-local, yet almost all existing methods rely on purely local differential descriptors and so miss where a point sits within the global shape. We identify three structural properties that current predictors systematically ignore: bilateral symmetry, contextual rank, and topological persistence. All three are learning-free and complement any local descriptor. Added to a strong multi-scale spiral-patch baseline on the Schelling dataset, our descriptor GSTF achieves the highest PLCC among published methods and an AUC competitive with the best. The gain survives a strict held-out-mesh test that prevents vertex-level leakage, and contextual rank provably carries no direct ground-truth signal.

Three learning-free feature families

Symmetry (SymF)

Spiral-patch distance to a vertex's bilateral mirror, weighted by a global symmetry-confidence term.

Contextual rank (CR)

Per-mesh empirical CDF of each base feature — a mesh-global sense of how a vertex ranks.

Persistence (Pers)

Persistence of each vertex under a curvature filtration, capturing topologically significant extrema.
