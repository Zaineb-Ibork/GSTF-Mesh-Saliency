Overview

Mesh saliency predicts which parts of a 3D surface draw human attention. Most predictors describe each vertex by local, differential features and cannot see cues that depend on the rest of the shape. We add three learning-free, non-local features to a multi-scale spiral-patch descriptor and study when and why they help.

Contextual rank (CR)

Where each feature value of a vertex falls within the distribution of its own mesh.

Persistence (Pers)

How far each curvature extremum stands out from its surroundings, from persistent homology.

Symmetry (SymF)

How a vertex's neighbourhood compares with that of its bilateral mirror, and a post-processing step that shares predictions between mirror vertices.

Conference paper

S+SSPR 2026
  • Introduces the three feature families on top of the spiral-patch baseline, with no change to the regressor.
  • On the Schelling benchmark, the full descriptor gives the highest correlation with the ground truth among published methods.
  • The gain holds on meshes held out from training, and contextual rank is shown to carry no direct information about the ground truth.

Journal extension

Under revision · coming soon

What we added

  • Evaluation under six named protocols: unseen meshes, unseen categories, training per category, decimated meshes, and the published split of a learned graph network, with paired statistical tests and error measures next to correlation.
  • Tests that replace each feature by a simpler signal, to find where its gain comes from.
  • A comparison with a learned graph neural network on its own test meshes, and the same network trained with our features as input.
  • A refined symmetry feature and plane estimation, a cross-database test on eye-tracking data, and the measured cost on new meshes.

What we found

  • Contextual rank and persistence improve the prediction on unseen meshes, unseen categories and decimated meshes.
  • Contextual rank works as a per-mesh normalisation; persistence helps through how prominent each extremum is, not only where it is.
  • Symmetry is most useful as a post-processing step.
  • With the two features, a small regressor outperforms a learned graph network on that network's own test meshes, and the features improve the network as well.
  • They add only a few percent to the cost of the descriptor.
